## Technical summary

**D-8 is reopened, although its central alarm-speed diagnosis survives.** My independent contract-B simulation did not reproduce the stronger claim that an event lowers alarm probability, but it did find weak alarm discrimination: at an approximately calibrated no-event mean run length of 1,042 steps, draft 2 alarmed within 200 steps in 10/30 event runs and 7/30 matched no-event windows. This is not persuasive detection power.

The new F1 primary has a more serious deterministic failure. In a valid `N_x=100`, 114-channel instance with four padding channels, six supported channels before the event and three after it, the draft-2 calibration split contains only 4.605% positive labels. Section 4 requires at least 5%, so the arm is forced to a constant predictor by construction. At threshold 0.5 it cannot beat another calibrated constant predictor on F1. Even when I deliberately did **not** enforce that fallback, sequential IBD's mean F1 at the primary offset 500 was 0.050 versus 0.000 for my explicit CUSUM mapping (20 seeds; paired delta SE 0.034), below the declared `delta_F1=0.10`. Therefore the interventional arm does not achieve the declared win in this permitted high-distractor cell.

At `N_x=10`, IBD did win relatively: mean F1 at offsets 200/500/1000 was 0.233/0.191/0.204 versus 0 throughout for the comparator (100 paired seeds; primary delta SE 0.024). That “win” is not validating evidence because the comparator's fitted isotonic map never exceeded 0.283 and therefore always predicted no support. IBD itself predicted only 3.3% of channels positive at offset 500 when the truth was 12.5%. **Opinion:** D-8 chose the right broad object—support correctness—but this frozen operationalisation can manufacture a relative win between two poor classifiers.

### Independent simulation scope

`review3-codex/independent_d8_simulation.py` implements a family-L instance with `N_b=4, N_d=2, N_w=4, K=2`, delay 0, contract noise scales, `N_x` 10 or 100, four padding channels, `f_conf=0.5`, a complete loss of actuator 0 at step 1,000, 5% block-periodic probing, the draft-2 windowed Mann–Whitney statistic, and 40 calibration episodes split evenly between event and no-event schedules. The lost actuator's direct effect is 0.35; its downstream body and `d` effects are 0.084 and 0.0875, all above `epsilon=0.05`. In a validation run, action saturation was 0.275% and maximum action–confounded-distractor correlation was 0.540, satisfying the frozen bounds.

The comparator uses a channel-agnostic linear predictor and an explicitly documented two-sided standardised innovation-shift score, then an arm-specific isotonic map fitted by the same protocol. This choice is a limitation, not a hidden degree of freedom: the frozen documents do not define the comparator's channel statistic or whether “the same calibrator” means the same algorithm or identical fitted knots. Alarm calibration used 80 no-event episodes and is only a qualitative check, not the contract's 400-run-length certification. Final row-level results are in `independent_d8_rows_nx10_two_sided.csv` and `independent_d8_rows_nx100_two_sided.csv`; the alarm result is in `independent_d8_alarm_summary_nx10.json`.

I also executed contract C4's family-L equation independently of the simulation: for `A_b=[0.6]`, `B=[0.7]`, `a=[1]` and `tau=1`, `open_loop_response` returned `0`, `0.7` and `0.42` at horizons 1, 2 and 3. These exactly match the declared piecewise form `M_{t,h}=0` for `h<=tau` and `A_b^(h-1-tau)B` otherwise.

I attempted to verify the draft's characterization of arXiv:2603.18257 live at both its abstract and HTML URLs, but the live browser returned no retrievable content. I therefore do not rely on or adjudicate that external characterization below.
