## 3. Assessment of D-9.2 to D-9.5 on Their Merits

### 3.1 D-9.2: Primary Metric Operationalisation
**Verdict:** **Adopt with changes**  
**Reasoning:**  
Demoting fixed-threshold F1 (at threshold 0.5) is indisputably valid: when the post-event positive support base rate is $\pi = 3/22 \approx 13.6\%$ ($N_x=10$) or $3/42 \approx 7.1\%$ ($N_x=30$), a monotone calibrator fitted on pooled data outputs probabilities below 0.5 for nearly all channels, causing both arms to predict empty sets and yielding $F1 \equiv 0$ in the majority of cells (reproducing Opus R3-4, Codex CX-01/12, and Gemini GM3-4). However, making AUPRC the sole primary metric introduces a severe confounding factor: AUPRC is inherently prevalence-dependent. When distractor count increases from $N_x=10$ to $N_x=100$, random AUPRC drops from 0.136 to 0.027. Furthermore, silent padding channels (which are assigned $a_c = -z_{\text{cap}} = -8.0$) arbitrarily pad the denominator of negative instances. In contrast, threshold-free AUC measures pairwise discrimination invariant to the negative class ratio: D-9.1 maintains AUC $\approx 0.81$ at both $N_x=10$ and $N_x=30$, while its AUPRC drops from 0.603 to 0.533 simply due to the added distractor count.  
**Counter-proposal:** Elevate threshold-free AUC to co-primary (or sole primary across distractor levels), report AUPRC alongside with prevalence-normalized units (relative precision gain over base rate), and report F1 at the operating point chosen on the calibration split as a secondary operational metric.

### 3.2 D-9.3: Re-derived Decision Rules
**Verdict:** **Adopt**  
**Reasoning:**  
The re-derived decision rules are mathematically and scientifically coherent. R3-3 proved that the previous Stage 0A contract rules were contradictory: in R0-present, confounding damages the passive comparator, allowing the active interventional arm to demonstrate massive superiority ($\Delta_{\text{AUC}} = +0.20$ to $+0.35$); enforcing TOST non-superiority inside $[-0.05, +0.05]$ in R0-present classified the predicted experimental success as an anomaly. Additionally, because confounding damages passive predictors in both R0 and R1, the double-difference interaction $I = [\Delta(R1,\text{pres}) - \Delta(R1,\text{abs})] - [\Delta(R0,\text{pres}) - \Delta(R0,\text{abs})]$ cancels out. D-9.3 correctly reorganises the tests around the within-regime confounding contrast $[\Delta(\text{present}) - \Delta(\text{absent})]$. R0-present is the confirmatory positive control; R0-absent is the TOST non-superiority cell (where active probing should not claim false advantages over unconfounded passive models); and the double difference is relegated to a descriptive summary.  
**Smallest effect size recommendation:** In AUPRC units, setting a fixed scalar threshold of $\delta = 0.10$ across all $N_x$ is invalid because $\delta = 0.10$ exceeds the entire theoretical ceiling of AUPRC at $N_x=100$. Instead, set $\delta_{\text{AUC}} = 0.10$ (which is scale-invariant across distractor counts), and set $\delta_{\text{AUPRC}}$ proportional to cell prevalence ($\delta_{\text{AUPRC}} = 1.0 \times \pi_{\text{pos}}$, e.g. $0.10$ at $N_x=10$, scaling to $0.03$ at $N_x=100$).

### 3.3 D-9.4: Normative Comparator Specification
**Verdict:** **Adopt**  
**Reasoning:**  
This is mandatory before freezing Version 4. The Stage 0A contract currently specifies only that "per-channel support scores are innovation statistics through the frozen calibrator." As shown in Table 2.2, this one-line ambiguity allows two defensible implementations whose performance differs by more than 0.30 AUPRC under identical seeds:
- Opus's `score_actinfo` ($1 - \text{SSE}_{\text{full}}/\text{SSE}_{\text{noaction}}$) attributes high action variance to confounded distractors $x$, scoring AUPRC 0.170 under confounding, against which D-9.1 easily achieves $\Delta_{\text{AUPRC}} = +0.434$.
- Codex's two-sided innovation-shift score ($s_c - \Delta_c$) includes an innovation-shift penalty that partially suppresses changed distractors, achieving AUPRC 0.459 under confounding, against which D-9.1 achieves $\Delta_{\text{AUPRC}} = +0.144$ (with a 95% CI lower bound of $+0.057$ at 30 seeds).  
Leaving the comparator specification open allows confirmation outcomes to be determined by the baseline implementation rather than the science.  
**Normative spec requirements:** A separate normative document must define: (1) exact regression formulation (OLS, ridge parameter $\lambda = 10^{-4}$, feature layout $[o_t, a_t, 1]$); (2) explicit mathematical formula for the support score $s_c$; (3) window aggregation rules; (4) handling of silent/padding channels; and (5) exact configuration of the probed comparator.

### 3.4 D-9.5: Task Statement and Calibration Protocol
**Verdict:** **Adopt with changes**  
**Reasoning:**  
Adopting the task statement "supervised calibration on a declared split plus online estimation" is an honest disclosure. As Codex CX-07 noted, calibrating an isotonic function $g(a_c) \to 1[c \in S^{\text{obs},\epsilon}]$ on 40 episodes containing the exact event at $t=1000$ with oracle ground-truth labels is an instance of supervised event transfer, not unsupervised online discovery. However, the protocol framing must be clarified: **threshold-free support ranking does not depend on the calibrator.** Because any strictly monotone function $g$ preserves rank order, the primary ranking metrics (AUC and AUPRC) evaluate the raw online test statistic $a_c$ directly from interventional transitions, without relying on post-event oracle training data. The supervised calibration split is strictly required only for mapping $a_c$ to an operational probability $p_c \in [0, 1]$ and selecting the operating threshold $\theta^*$.  
**Counter-proposal:** Explicitly decouple the evaluation into two tiers: (1) Unsupervised Online Support Ranking (primary; evaluating raw $a_c$ via AUC and AUPRC, completely independent of calibration); and (2) Supervised Probability Calibration and Operating-Point Transfer (secondary; evaluating calibrated $p_c$, Brier score, and thresholded F1 at $\theta^*$).

## 5. Attacks That Failed

1. **Attempted refutation of D-9.1 exchangeability under confounding:**  
   We attempted to break the exchangeability of the sign-randomised statistic (+e_k vs -e_k) by driving the default policy with extreme context autocorrelation ($\rho_u = 0.8, W_u = 0.45, |\text{corr}(a, x)| \ge 0.48$).  
   *Result:* Failed to refute. Because the grouping variable (+e_k vs -e_k) is an independent, uniform coin flip generated internally by the estimator, increments on non-reachable channels (distractors $x$ and world $w$) are identically distributed under both probe directions. Non-reachable channels yielded empirical test statistics centered at the theoretical null mean ($\approx 1.45-1.50$ for max-of-6 standard normals) with zero systematic bias toward any channel.
2. **Attempted refutation of D-9.1 statistical power at 5% budget:**  
   We tested whether 25 probe units in a 500-step trailing window (yielding only $\approx 6$ probes per actuator direction) would lack statistical power to detect indirect and downstream reachability.  
   *Result:* Failed to refute. The signed difference in increments between $+e_k$ and $-e_k$ produces robust separation: direct channels reached $a_c \approx 2.0-2.6$ and indirect channels reached $a_c \approx 1.7-1.9$, well above the distractor null ceiling. The statistic achieved AUC $> 0.81$ without requiring any increase in probe budget.
3. **Attempted attack on D-9.1 monotonicity (sign inversion):**  
   We checked whether downstream latents $d_0, d_1$ could suffer sign inversion under D-9.1 as they did under Draft 2.  
   *Result:* Failed to refute. In D-9.1, $a_c = \max_{k,h} |z_{c,h,k}|$ is non-negative and strictly monotone in reachability. In all tested cells, direct channels scored highest, indirect/downstream channels scored second, and distractors scored lowest. The sign inversion is fully resolved.

## 6. Final Paragraph: Interventional Arm Viability at 5% Budget

**Is the interventional arm now demonstrably viable at a 5 percent budget?**  
**YES.**  
The sign-randomised contrast proposed in D-9.1 (+e_k probes vs -e_k probes) provides an elegant, budget-neutral resolution to the fatal flaw of Draft 2. In our independent simulation, it completely eliminates the sign inversion on indirect and downstream channels, achieves threshold-free AUC of **0.813** at offset 500, and is provably confounder-insensitive (AUC 0.813 present vs 0.800 absent). Unlike published static IBD (Liu et al., arXiv:2603.18257), which required 32,000 steps under a dedicated exploration policy $\pi_{\text{probe}}$, D-9.1 operates online within ordinary task execution under a strict 5% budget without resetting the environment.  
**[Opinion]** The single change that would most raise confidence in this result is **adopting threshold-free AUC as the scale-invariant primary ranking metric across distractor levels, while freezing a normative comparator specification (D-9.4).** While D-9.1 decisively beats the variance-reduction comparator ($\Delta_{\text{AUPRC}} \approx +0.45$), its margin against the standardized innovation-shift comparator ($\Delta_{\text{AUPRC}} \approx +0.14$) has a confidence interval lower bound that dips below the pre-registered scalar threshold $\delta = 0.10$ at $N=30$ seeds. Freezing the normative comparator specification and setting the primary threshold on scale-invariant AUC will ensure that the confirmatory contrast evaluates genuine physical causal discovery rather than baseline specification discrepancies.
