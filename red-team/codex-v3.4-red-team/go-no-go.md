The benchmark direction is worth executing, but Stage 0A should not start from contract v2.2. “Unconditional go” is unsupported.

Before scaffolding:

1. **Replace proof theatre with a gate.** Turn the print-only scripts into assertions, implement mutants M1–M6, publish a contract-to-test coverage matrix, and make violations return non-zero.
2. **Repair the experiment.** Complete loss in a single-actuator system cannot be feedback-masked. Use complete loss for support detection in R0/R1 and partial loss or redundant actuation for R2, scored as an action-effect change.
3. **Repair the statistic.** Incorporate censored detections, freeze a common delay range, and stop converting disjoint operating curves into an incomparable boundary scalar.
4. **Repair the oracle.** Constrain stability/stationarity, include gain in operational observed support, and quantify numerical uncertainty for nonlinear labels instead of calling them exact.
5. **Consolidate the plan.** Replace the four-layer amendment stack with one roadmap, one contract and a machine-readable confirmation matrix naming the exact CUSUM/GLR comparator.
6. **Redesign Paper 2’s treatment.** Route noncontrollable but task-relevant inputs to an exogenous branch; do not delete everything outside controllable support.

After those changes, Stage 0A is a **go**. Paper 1 remains a plausible benchmark/failure-map contribution. Paper 2 remains promising, but its novelty is a cross-architecture changing-boundary stress test and successful routing remedy—not first discovery of confounded action attribution.
