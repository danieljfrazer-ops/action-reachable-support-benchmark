## 3. Convergence and the D-10 exit condition

**Two-of-three or better, and therefore adoptable under the convergence rule:** the D-10.3 floor failure (all three); CL-4 / `s_change` not certified (all three); cross-process non-determinism from the salted `hash` (all three); surviving `reference_generator` mutants including the `c_min` violation, the invertible confounded mask and the dropped CRN key (all three); the 99-probe clock discrepancy (Claude, Gemini); the ARL_0 / warm-up protocol gap (all three, from three different angles); the retired estimator ids and the stale precedence chain (Claude, Codex); the undefined fit hierarchy (Claude, Codex); the undefined probe-RNG lifecycle (Claude, Codex); family L only (Claude, Codex).

**Single-vote, parked with dissent recorded:** my R5-3 / R5-4 (the comparator's score is dominated by its own baseline term; a fit-time constant beats both arms on the primary) — though Codex's R5-CX-05 is the same defect derived theoretically, so I would argue this is two-of-three on substance; Codex's R5-CX-10, R5-CX-15 and R5-CX-16, which I now endorse, making them two-of-three; my R5-15 (degeneracy and `q_cap` unreachable), R5-16 (asymmetric calibrator purity), R5-27 (`N_x` levels share body dynamics), R5-29 (benefit decomposition).

**Judgment calls for Daniel:** whether the perturbation set is a robustness gate or descriptive (R5-CX-04); whether ineligible cells should score 0 or be omitted (R5-GM-07); whether to hold out fresh confirmation seeds now (R5-CX-16); and, above all, what to do about R5-2 — the contract says the phase stops, and R5-3 / R5-CX-05 say why.

**The D-10 exit condition — "three models on the frozen configuration set and perturbation set agree to Monte Carlo error and return one margin verdict" — is NOT met.**
- The three implementations **do** return one margin verdict, and it is the same one: the confounding benefit clears δ_AUC = 0.10 in the four base cells at the primary offset, and the D-10.3 floor that guards it fails in all four. That much is convergent.
- They do **not** agree to Monte Carlo error everywhere. On the base cells agreement is good (comparator-absent lower bounds at offset 500: Claude 0.829 / 0.774 / 0.829 / 0.772, Codex 0.824 / 0.767 / 0.822 / 0.765, Gemini 0.810 / 0.710 / 0.813 / 0.710). On the single-instance perturbation reference cell it is poor, and the split is diagnostic: the **comparator** reproduces to three decimals between Codex and me (0.825 present, 0.925 absent) while the **IBD** arm does not (0.962 / 0.887 / 0.784). The arm with no unspecified randomness agrees; the arm whose probe RNG the specs never seed does not. Add the process-salted noise key (R5-1) on top, and the exit condition is not currently *testable*, let alone met.
