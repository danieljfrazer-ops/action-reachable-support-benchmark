## Task 1 — contract equation verified by independent execution

`verify_contract.py`, run with the pyenv 3.12.0 interpreter: **106/106 checks passed.**
It does **not** import `contract_ref.py` or `test_gate.py`. It re-derives the quantities from the
contract prose:

- **C4 family-L closed form.** I brute-force-simulated contract section B's body equation
  `b_{t+1} = A_b b_t + B_t a_{t-tau} + eps^b_t` with an explicit action-history buffer, applied
  `do(a_0 = a, a_{1..h-1} = 0)` against `do(0)` under **common random numbers**, and compared the
  difference against `M = 0 for h <= tau, A_b^{h-1-tau} B_t for h > tau`. Over 4 random instances x
  tau in {0,1,2,3} x h in {1..6} (96 cells, rectangular and square B, rho(A_b) = 0.9): agreement to
  <= 4.4e-16. The delay semantics ("first hit at h = tau+1") are correct as written.
- **C2/C4 as an expectation.** With CRN the paired estimator has zero variance
  (sd ~ 6e-14); with independent noise it is unbiased at n_oracle = 4096 (mean [0.8521, 0.3589]
  vs truth [0.85, 0.35], se ~ 0.0097). The contract's insistence on CRN is quantitatively justified.
- **C1 reachability.** An independently written graph-reachability routine agreed with a
  zero-noise finite-difference derivation over the full latent stack `[b; d; w; x]` on
  200 instances x tau in {0,1,2} x H in {1..4} — 0 mismatches (and 0/3200 on sign-mixed instances).
- **C3 / K8 and G / HPDT** recomputed by hand: all agree.

**Conclusion: the contract's mathematics is right.** My findings are about the gate's
*discriminating power*, the statistics, the interface, and the T2 arm — not the algebra.

## Attacks that failed

Recorded because a failed attack is evidence for the plan.

1. **The contract's algebra.** I tried to break C1, C4 and C3 by independent brute-force simulation
   (`verify_contract.py`, 106 checks). The delay semantics (`first hit at h = tau+1`), the piecewise
   Jacobian `A^{h-1-tau}B`, the zeroing for `h <= tau`, rectangular B row-indexing, the gain-inclusive
   S^obs,eps, and the HPDT arithmetic are all exactly right. The CRN requirement is quantitatively
   justified (zero-variance vs se ~ 0.01 at n = 4096). This is unusually solid for a plan document.
2. **Path cancellation breaking T-E1b.** I predicted graph reachability on sign patterns would
   disagree with the finite-difference derivation when two paths cancel, and that `c_min = 0.2`
   would not prevent it. **0 mismatches in 3200 sign-mixed instance x tau x H combinations**
   (`verify_contract.py` V3b). Exact cancellation is measure-zero under continuous sampling.
   *Near*-cancellation is also rarer than I expected: `attacks.py` A8 finds P(min response over
   reachable components < 0.05) = **1.3%** at N_b = 4 and **0.1%** at N_b = 6-8. The eps_faith
   resample rate is therefore probably nowhere near the 20% cap; OP-25 survives only as
   "the constant has no value", not as "the generator will thrash".
3. **"It is too slow for a fanless MacBook Air."** I set out to show the run matrix was
   computationally infeasible and the measurement refuted me. Bare family-L stepping runs at
   **168,190 steps/s** on this arm64 host (`throughput.py`); even 1.13e9 steps is ~1.9 h. A fully
   vectorised C2 certification with n_oracle = 4096 over all (component, horizon, probe) triples
   takes **12-76 ms**, i.e. 22-135 s across all 1764 rows for one event each (`attacks.py` A9).
   Compute is not the binding constraint for the SCM arm; I withdrew the claim and rewrote OP-8 as
   a statistical inconsistency. The residual runtime risks are the T2 arm (Python-level Gymnasium
   stepping, MLP residual models per contract F) and the ~19x calibration inflation in OP-5 —
   which is why the runtime pilot (contract I, decision D5) is the right call.
4. **Mutating the delay semantics.** Off-by-one attacks in both directions (OP-M10 over-reach,
   OP-M11 early-arriving Jacobian) were killed by 3 and 1 tests respectively. `test_C1_delay_*`,
   `test_C1_chain_*` and `test_C4_open_loop_*` genuinely pin tau.
5. **Mutating `avail` and `gain` out of C3.** Both killed (OP-M13, OP-M14). `test_K5` and `test_L5`
   do their job; the coverage claim for C3's *observable* terms is real. Only the padding guard
   and the strict inequality are unpinned (OP-M4, OP-M5).
6. **Checking the Paper 2 citations.** All four named architectures exist and are described
   accurately enough for a design document: Iso-Dream (NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2205.13817),
   Denoised MDP, Dueling World Models (arXiv 2608.06706), Sensorimotor World Models
   (arXiv 2606.20104, Ivashkov/Balestriero/Scholkopf). No finding.
7. **Checking the IBD characterisation.** Contract H's parenthetical "~32k-step probe phase" is
   **exactly right** (2NT = 2*80*200), and "sequential re-probing is new engineering and is
   labelled as such" is an honest statement of the gap — the paper says outright that its
   "setting assumes stationary probing". The plan is not misrepresenting its comparator. The
   finding (OP-8) is about the arithmetic of the frozen constants, not about the citation.
8. **Trying to make an unstable instance diverge.** My first version of OP-16 claimed the
   b -> d -> o -> a -> b loop would cause divergence. It does not: the a_max clip is enough to
   keep the latents bounded. I corrected the script and the finding to the (still fatal but
   different) saturation failure mode. Noted here because the original claim was wrong.
