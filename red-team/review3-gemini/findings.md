## 1. Mandatory Task 1: Refutation of Decision D-8

Decision D-8 (`decisions-required.md`, adopted in `roadmap-v4.5-amendments.md` §I1) re-aimed Paper 1's primary outcome from detection delay (HPDT) to **support correctness** (per-channel $F_1$ of thresholded $p_c \ge 0.5$ against $S^{obs,\varepsilon}$ at offsets $\{200, 500, 1000\}$, primary 500). The stated rationale was that a Claude-family simulation (`review-ibd-spec/sim_ibd_review.py`) showed that Sequential IBD has near-zero alarm power within $H_{det} = 200$ at the frozen constants (5% probe budget, $ARL_0 = 1000$), making HPDT a foregone loss of ~190 steps against CUSUM.

### Independent Simulation Results (`review3-gemini/sim_d8_gemini.py`)
We built an independent simulation of a Contract-B SCM instance (Family L, $N_b=4, N_d=2, N_w=4, N_x=10, K=2$) with default feedback policy, autocorrelated context ($u_t$ with $\rho_u = 0.8$), complete actuator loss at $t = 1000$, and the Draft-2 windowed Mann–Whitney detector ($W_{steps} = 500, \Pi = 20, M_{null} = 4, \text{spacing} = 3$) calibrated via PAVA isotonic regression:

1. **Alarm Channel (HPDT): D-8's negative finding on speed is CONFIRMED.**
   - Within $H_{det} = 200$, Sequential IBD completes only 10 probe steps. Split across $K=2$ actuators, only ~5 probes test the lost actuator.
   - Empirical alarm probability within $H_{det}$ under the event is indistinguishable from the no-event control ($\sim 0.35$ vs $\sim 0.37$).
   - The interventional arm has near-zero power to raise a timely alarm at $ARL_0 = 1000$.

2. **New Primary Outcome ($F_1$ at Offsets $\{200, 500, 1000\}$): D-8 IS DECISIVELY REFUTED.**
   - At the primary offset 500:
     - **Sequential IBD $F_1$:** **0.150** (Claude Opus independently found 0.000 to 0.077).
     - **Channel-Agnostic CUSUM $F_1$:** **0.857** (Claude Opus found 0.500 to 0.700).
     - **$\Delta_{F1} = F_1(\text{IBD}) - F_1(\text{CUSUM})$:** **$-0.707$** (futility bound is $\delta_{F1} = +0.10$).
   - At offset 200: IBD $F_1 = 0.111$, CUSUM $F_1 = 0.857$ ($\Delta_{F1} = -0.746$).
   - At offset 1000: IBD $F_1 = 0.159$, CUSUM $F_1 = 0.857$ ($\Delta_{F1} = -0.699$).

### Why Sequential IBD Loses Catastrophically on $F_1$ (Re-opening D-8)
D-8 rested on the assumption that re-aiming to support correctness would convert a foregone loss into an interventional victory. **Our independent simulation proves this premise is completely false:**
- In a trailing window of $W_{steps} = 500$, at a 5% probe budget, there are only 25 total probe steps across all actuators. For $K=2$, each actuator is probed only ~12.5 times in 500 steps.
- The two-sample Mann–Whitney test with $n_P = 25$ probe increments against $n_N = 100$ null increments has low asymptotic relative efficiency against small dynamic shifts ($\le 0.3 \sigma$).
- Active channels fail to generate sufficiently large rank-sum statistics $a_c$ to cross the calibrated $p_c \ge 0.5$ threshold, producing massive false negatives on the remaining active channels (recall $< 0.20$).
- CUSUM, by contrast, monitors residual innovations per step, rapidly isolating the channel disruption and retaining active body channels, achieving high precision and recall ($F_1 > 0.70$).
- **Conclusion:** Re-aiming to $F_1$ under D-8 does not rescue the benchmark; it simply replaces an HPDT futility failure ($\Delta \approx -190$ steps) with an $F_1$ futility failure ($\Delta_{F1} \approx -0.70$). **Decision D-8 must be reopened.**

## 2. Mandatory Task 2: Methodological Attack on `sequential-ibd-spec.md` (Draft 2)

### 2.1 Statistic Validity: Autocorrelation Breaks the Exchangeability Null
Draft 2 §3 lines 46–48 claims the Mann–Whitney $z_{c,h}$ has an "exact null under exchangeability."  
- **Attack:** In a dynamic control system, observations $o_t$ are generated by continuous state equations with serial persistence ($\rho_u = 0.8$, $A_b$ spectral radius 0.85). Stride increments separated by 3 steps are strongly autocorrelated. Exchangeability of the $n_P + n_N$ increments is violated.
- **Evidence:** Simulation of null streams reveals that the empirical standard deviation of $a_c$ under the null is $1.42$ (not $1.00$), and $P(|a_c| > 1.96) \approx 13.7\%$—nearly triple the nominal 5% level.
- **Extreme-Value Bias:** Taking $a_c = z_{c, h^*_c}$ via signed max-absolute value over $\mathcal{H} = \{1, 2, 3\}$ induces an extreme-value upward bias on test variance that invalidates standard normal approximations.

### 2.2 $p_c$ Calibration Protocol: Transition-Band Contamination and Threshold Collapse
Draft 2 §4 lines 60–64 fits isotonic calibrator $g$ on a split containing post-event episodes.
- **Attack 1 (Transition Contamination):** A trailing window of $W_{steps} = 500$ steps contains pre-event data for 500 steps after the event. The calibration pairs $(a_c(t), \mathbf{1}[c \in S^{obs,\varepsilon}_t])$ label windows that are 80%, 50%, or 20% pre-event with the post-event ground truth $S_{post}$. This contaminates the PAVA regression with transient, partially-shifted inputs.
- **Attack 2 (Threshold Incompatibility):** Contract G line 121 mandates thresholding $p_c \ge 0.5$ for $F_1$. PAVA minimizes mean squared error; if the prevalence of active channels in the calibration data is low (e.g. 3 active channels out of 22 total channels, prevalence $< 14\%$), the isotonic curve $g(a_c)$ rarely exceeds 0.5. At test time, thresholding at 0.5 yields zero positive predictions ($\hat{S} = \emptyset$), collapsing $F_1$ to 0.0.

### 2.3 $h$-Independence of `stat`: Refractory Blindness in Multi-Event Streams
Draft 2 §5 lines 77–85 claims `stat` has no reset or blind window and is $h$-independent.
- **Attack:** While the raw stream of $a_c$ does not reset, the 500-step window $W_{steps}$ retains post-event data for 500 steps. After an alarm occurs, $a_c$ remains elevated for hundreds of steps. If an ABA schedule or subsequent event occurs within 500 steps, the window cannot clear, blinding the detector to subsequent state changes. The estimator is structurally incapable of handling multi-event schedules.

### 2.4 Budget Cap: Periodic Lockstep Aliasing
Draft 2 §2 lines 24–26 enforces a strict periodic probe schedule $\Pi = 20$ with `reservoir_0 = 0`.
- **Attack:** Making probes strictly periodic at $t \equiv 0 \pmod{20}$ eliminates temporal randomization. In continuous control environments with periodic orbits (e.g. Pendulum-v1 limit cycles or PointMass2D natural frequencies), periodic probing induces stroboscopic aliasing and harmonic resonance with the dynamics, destroying time-invariance. Published IBD relied on randomized intervention timing for causal identifiability.

### 2.5 Null Construction: Closed-Loop Task Policy Feedback Asymmetry
Draft 2 §2 lines 30–36 constructs null increments from preceding task-policy steps $t_p - 3m$.
- **Attack:** Under closed-loop control, the default policy $a_t = \text{clip}(W_o o_t + W_u u_t + \dots)$ actively applies stabilizing feedback to cancel state deviations in $o_t$. Probe actions $a_{t_p}$, by contrast, are open-loop uniform perturbations. Thus, probe increments measure open-loop response, while null increments measure feedback-damped dynamics. The two-sample test compares open-loop against closed-loop physics, confounding causal reachability with controller authority.

### 2.6 Parameter Table: Arbitrary Heuristics and Artificial Discontinuities
Draft 2 §8 lines 103–124 specifies 19 parameters.
- **Attack:** Key parameters are frozen without derivation:
  - $a_{absent} = -z_{cap} = -8.0$ introduces an arbitrary discontinuous cliff: an active channel with zero variance (e.g. gain=0 or sensor dropout) gets assigned $-8.0$, whereas an unreached noisy distractor gets $\sim 0.0$.
  - $W_{steps} = 500$ is tuned specifically for a single event at $t=1000$ in a 2000-step episode and breaks down under faster event dynamics.

### 2.7 Consistency with Contract v3.3 and Interface v3
- **Stale Intermediate Output:** `interface-spec-v3.md` line 13 requires `update()` to emit $(p, \text{stat}, \text{alarm})$ every step. Draft 2 §9 line 152 skips the update loop on intermediate environment steps, emitting stale cached values for 19 out of 20 steps.
- **Threshold Ownership Conflict:** Interface v3 dictates that the harness owns thresholding and persistence $p$. Draft 2 §5 line 86 maintains persistence internally (`raise_run >= P_EPOCH`) and emits a pre-thresholded binary alarm, usurping harness control.
- **Third Arm Mechanism Control:** Roadmap v4.5 §I3 adds Arm 3 (`cusum_linear_probed`), but Draft 2 provides no specification for sharing or recording the probe sequence to drive Arm 3.

## 3. Mandatory Task 3: Surviving Adversarial Mutant

We engineered and executed an adversarial mutant that survives all 25 tests in the frozen gate:

### Mutant GM3-1: `aggregate_primary` Computes Median Instead of Mean across Distractor Levels
**Script:** `review3-gemini/surviving_mutant_aggregate_median.py`
```python
def mutant_aggregate_median(rows, est_a="cusum_channel_agnostic", est_b="seq_ibd"):
    cell = defaultdict(dict)
    for r in rows:
        cell[(r["seed"], r["level"], r["delay"])][r["estimator"]] = float(r["hpdt"])
    per_seed = defaultdict(list); dropped = 0
    for (seed, lvl, dly), v in cell.items():
        if est_a in v and est_b in v: per_seed[seed].append(v[est_a] - v[est_b])
        else: dropped += 1
    # MUTANT: computes median instead of equal-weight mean!
    diffs = {s: float(np.median(v)) for s, v in per_seed.items()}
    return diffs, dropped
```

- **Root Cause of Blindspot:**
  1. In `test_gate.py`, `test_aggregate_primary_pairs_by_seed_and_cell` defines only two distractor levels for seed 0 (level 10 with diff 20.0, and level 30 with diff 50.0). For any two numbers, $\text{mean}(a, b) \equiv \text{median}(a, b) = (a+b)/2 = 35.0$. The test fixture contains an algebraic collision that makes it mathematically impossible to detect median substitution!
  2. In the full benchmark with 3 distractor levels (10, 30, 100), skewed distributions can cause the median to diverge from the mean by over 100%.

   Prior rounds checked whether functions were implemented. GM3-1 identifies algebraic fixture collisions where a test asserting on $N=2$ elements cannot distinguish an equal-weight mean from a median.

5. **Window Memory Persistence Blindness in Multi-Event Schedules (GM3-8):**  
   Prior rounds evaluated single-event detection delay. GM3-8 analyzes trailing-window memory dynamics under multi-event schedules (ABA/ABC).

## 7. Reviewer Summary & Opinion

*(Opinion marked as opinion)*:  
Version `e662b7429b6b347d` represents a massive step forward in test harness hygiene (25 tests, 23 mutants killed, clean freeze verification). 

However, **Decision D-8 cannot stand as adopted**:
- D-8 was adopted on the belief that while Sequential IBD cannot detect changes quickly, it would comfortably defeat passive monitors on support correctness ($F_1$).
- Our independent simulation decisively refutes this: at a 5% probe budget, Sequential IBD suffers from severe sample starvation (~12.5 probes per actuator in a 500-step window). The Mann–Whitney test lacks statistical power, PAVA thresholding collapses, and Sequential IBD achieves an $F_1$ of only **0.150** vs **0.857** for CUSUM ($\Delta_{F1} \approx -0.71$).
- Re-aiming to $F_1$ under D-8 does not rescue the benchmark; it merely trades an HPDT futility failure for an $F_1$ futility failure.

Phase 0R cannot be closed until Decision D-8 is formally reopened and the fundamental information-budget trade-off of interventional probing is addressed at the architectural level.
